## 1. Project Name
MeltWise: AI Energy Copilot for India's Foundries

Smart Manufacturing (Challenge 04: Industrial Energy & Process Efficiency)

## 3. Project Description (457 words; the limit is 500)

India is the world's second-largest producer of castings, with about 5,000 foundries, 90% of them MSMEs, supplying the automotive, railway, pump and agricultural-equipment industries. In foundries that melt iron in electric induction furnaces, melting is typically the largest electricity load, and energy can make up 15–30% of production cost. Yet studies of Indian foundries find melting energy running 13–21% above industry norms.

The waste is hidden inside every melt. A Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) cluster study found the same furnace swinging by 50–80 kWh per tonne between melts, driven by operating practice: overheating the metal, holding it while the moulding line is down, and running at part power. Open lids and sandy or rusty scrap add more. The best melts happen by accident, and a monthly electricity bill cannot show which melt wasted energy or why. Small foundries have no energy manager, patchy internet and little capital, so systems built for large plants do not fit them.

MeltWise is an AI energy copilot for induction-furnace foundries. A low-cost kit reads the furnace's existing panel meter, or a Modbus energy meter, through an offline-first edge gateway, while operators log charge weight, scrap mix and temperature on a simple phone app in their own language. Our software splits each melt's power curve into phases, uses a physics-informed machine-learning model to predict how much energy that particular charge should need, and flags the cause of any excess while the melt is still running, for example "lid open 3 minutes, about 3 kWh lost" or "target temperature reached, tap now". A scheduler aligns melts with moulding-line readiness to cut holding time and moves melting into cheaper solar-hour time-of-day tariffs within the plant's contract demand. Owners receive a monthly savings report and a per-batch record of energy and CO2 per tonne, aligned with the GHG Protocol, that they can share with buyers.

What is new is the level of detail and the audience. Existing tools report monthly totals to managers; MeltWise diagnoses every melt in real time and explains it to the person standing at the furnace. It is designed for 500 kg to 5 tonne furnaces and for small-foundry realities.

For a typical foundry melting 2,400 tonnes a year at 650 kWh per tonne, our model estimates savings of 17–33 kWh per tonne (2.6–5.1%), 40–80 MWh and ₹3.7–9.5 lakh a year including tariff shifting, and about 28–57 tonnes of CO2 avoided, with a payback of one to five months. Throughput and quality are protected: tapping temperatures stay within specification and melt cycles become shorter. Across 1,000 foundries, the likely case adds up to about 80 GWh and 57,000 tonnes of CO2 a year. Beneficiaries include foundry owners, furnace operators and the manufacturing supply chains that depend on competitive Indian castings.
