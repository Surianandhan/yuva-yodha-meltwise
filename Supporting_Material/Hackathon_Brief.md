## Our challenge: 04 Smart Manufacturing (Industrial Energy & Process Efficiency)
**In one line:** bring affordable real-time energy monitoring, predictive maintenance and process optimisation to Indian SMEs, so they cut energy use and emissions without losing output, quality or competitiveness.

**The organiser's context figures:**
- Industry uses about 35–40% of India's energy.
- In energy-intensive SMEs, energy can be 15–30% of production cost. Examples include foundries, textile mills, ceramics, chemicals, food processing and brick kilns.
- Many SMEs have no real-time energy monitoring.
- India's industrial energy-efficiency scheme (PAT) and BEE norms cover large industry, but most SMEs fall outside them.
- Global buyers increasingly demand carbon transparency from suppliers.

**What the solution should achieve:**
1. Reduce specific energy consumption (energy per unit of output) without cutting throughput or quality.
2. Give operators and managers real-time, actionable visibility of energy, equipment health and process parameters, using affordable and easy-to-deploy tools.
3. Identify and quantify ways to cut direct and indirect emissions, including fuel switching and renewables.
4. Be economically viable for SMEs with limited capital and technical staff.

**What to deliver** (full detail is expected at the prototype stage):
- A write-up covering the mechanism, key assumptions and fit with Indian SME conditions
- An architecture diagram showing the sensors, edge or cloud software, data flows, and integration with plant equipment or ERP
- Design artefacts: dashboards or wireframes, data models, process and deployment schematics
- A software prototype or simulation (encouraged but optional; no hardware expected)
- A quantified improvement in specific energy consumption or emissions intensity against a clear baseline, with quality and throughput preserved
- A deployment and business-model plan covering the target segment, installation cost, payback period and how to scale across Indian SMEs

**The organiser's example directions:**
- IoT energy monitoring with an edge dashboard
- AI predictive maintenance for motors, compressors and furnaces
- Scheduling that follows tariffs and renewable supply
- A digital energy audit against BEE norms
- Carbon accounting aligned with the GHG Protocol

## Other challenges (for reference)
- **01 Sustainable Agriculture (energy, water & productivity):** distributed solar, precision irrigation, low-cost sensing and tools for farmers. The goals are less energy and water per unit of output, fewer post-harvest losses, support for smallholders, and climate resilience.
- **02 Smart Buildings (energy efficiency & occupant experience):** sensing, controls, analytics and loads that respond to the grid. The goals are less waste, better comfort and air quality, data-driven management, and peak shifting / demand response.
- **03 Grid Reliability & Renewable Intermittency:** neighbourhood-scale flexibility through forecasting, load management, demand response and shared storage. The goals are covering supply gaps locally, affordability for low-income areas, and data or signals for the local electricity distributors (DISCOMs).
