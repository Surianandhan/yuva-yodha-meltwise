## Where the savings come from (kWh saved per tonne)
Each lever is a shop-floor practice that MeltWise detects and corrects, melt by melt. The physics conversions are:
- overshoot: 0.30 kWh/t per °C (E1)
- lid left open: 1.0 kWh/min (E2)
- holding or under-powered time: 1.0 kWh/min (E3)
- sand in the charge: 1.1 kWh per kg (F9)

| Lever | Baseline practice | Conservative | Likely | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature overshoot above spec | 40 °C | −15 °C → 4.4 | −25 °C → 7.4 | −30 °C → 8.9 |
| Holding molten metal | 15 min/heat | −4 min → 4.0 | −8 min → 8.0 | −11 min → 11.0 |
| Lid left open (avoidable) | 6 min/heat | −2 min → 2.0 | −4 min → 4.0 | −5 min → 5.0 |
| Under-powered melting (extra time) | 8 min/heat | −3 min → 3.0 | −5 min → 5.0 | −7 min → 7.0 |
| Sand and rust in charge | 15 kg/t | −3 kg → 3.3 | −8 kg → 8.8 | −10 kg → 11.0 |
| **Total** | **≈57 kWh/t avoidable** | **16.7** | **33.2** | **42.9** |

**Cross-checks from BEE's foundry-cluster study:**
- A basic kWh indicator-cum-integrator alone saves **20 kWh/t** (F4). Our conservative case is *below* that.
- The same furnace varies **50–80 kWh/t between heats** (F3). Our baseline's avoidable waste (≈57 kWh/t) sits inside that range. Even the stretch case recovers only 75% of it.

These savings count **only** as cost, and **not** as CO₂, because India publishes no official hourly grid emission factors.

**Scale check.** 1,000 foundries × 2,400 t is 2.4 Mt of liquid metal a year. India produced 15.86 Mt of castings in 2024–25 (F1), and 1,000 foundries is about 20% of the ~5,000 units.

## Quality and throughput are preserved
- **Tapping temperature:** MeltWise removes overshoot *above* each grade's specified temperature and never targets below it. Metallurgy and casting quality are unchanged.
- **Cycle time:** less holding and full-power melting shorten each heat by 7–18 minutes. Throughput can only stay the same or rise.
- **Charge:** cleaner charge (less sand and rust) also means less slag, which helps quality.

## Upside we do not count
- Predictive maintenance, such as avoided coil and refractory failures
- Peak-demand charge reductions
- Equipment upgrades, such as IGBT furnaces or preheating, financed through SIDBI 4E (F14). MeltWise's measured baseline makes these loans easier to justify.
