## Engineering estimates (validate in pilot)
| Tag | Estimate | Working |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | Each °C of overshoot costs about **0.30 kWh/t** (about 3 kWh/t per 10 °C). | Liquid iron specific heat is about 0.82 kJ/kg·K, or 0.228 kWh/t·K. Overall efficiency on a molten bath is about 0.97 × 0.80 ≈ **0.77** (F7, F17), so 0.228 ÷ 0.77 ≈ 0.30. This leaves out the extra radiation at higher temperature (F9), so it errs low. |
| E2 | An open lid costs about **1.0 kWh per minute per tonne** in a 1 t furnace. | The bath is about 0.5 m across (0.19 m²). Black-body radiation at 1,500 °C is about 560 kW/m², and with an emissivity of about 0.4 the loss is about 43 kW of heat. At 77% efficiency that is about 56 kW of electricity, or about 0.9 kWh/min. The model rounds to 1.0. |
| E3 | Holding or slow melting costs about **1.0 kWh per minute per tonne** in time-dependent losses. | Heat conducts through about 100 mm of lining (k ≈ 2 W/m·K, ΔT ≈ 1,400 K, area ≈ 1.35 m²) to the water-cooled coil, about 38 kW. Radiation through lid gaps adds about 6–9 kW. That totals about 45 kW of heat, or about 58 kW of electricity (~1.0 kWh/min). |
| E4 | Model plant, one 1 t furnace:<br>• 8 heats a day × 300 days = **2,400 t/yr** of liquid metal<br>• 75-minute heat cycle: 52 min melting, 8 min under-powered, 15 min holding<br>Baseline gaps in practice:<br>• 40 °C of overshoot<br>• 15 min of holding<br>• 6 min of avoidable lid-open time<br>• 8 min under-powered<br>• 15 kg/t of sand and rust | Together these gaps come to about **57 kWh/t** of avoidable loss. That sits inside BEE's observed 50–80 kWh/t spread between heats (F3). The scenarios recover 29% (conservative), 58% (likely) or 75% (stretch) of it. |

## Claims we deliberately do not make
- **We don't say CBAM covers castings.** It mostly doesn't (F15), so we cite "buyer carbon-disclosure requests (GHG Protocol)" instead.
- **We don't say "most Indian foundries use induction furnaces".** We found no reliable national share, and many still run cupolas.
- **We don't count emission cuts from moving load into solar hours.** India publishes no official hourly grid emission factor, so ToD shifting is counted as a **cost** saving only.
- **We don't count savings from predictive maintenance or from equipment upgrades.** These are upside, not claims.
